About the forum

A national platform, not another conference

Executive summary

Zimbabwe stands at a critical point where economic transformation, industrial competitiveness, and innovation must converge to unlock sustainable growth. Across industries, businesses face operational inefficiencies, rising costs, technological disruption, supply-chain challenges, and increasing pressure to modernise. At the same time, Zimbabwe possesses a growing pool of talented innovators, startups, researchers, universities, and technology developers whose ideas often remain disconnected from real market opportunities.

The Zimbabwe Business Innovation Forum (ZBIF) bridges that gap. Unlike conventional conferences focused mainly on discussion, ZBIF is a solution-driven marketplace: industry challenges are sourced before the event and assigned to innovators, universities, startups and research institutions to solve. At the forum, innovators present commercially viable solutions directly to industry leaders, investors and policymakers.

Success is measured not by attendance, but by innovations adopted, partnerships formed, pilot projects initiated, commercial agreements signed, and investment unlocked.

Why now

Zimbabwean industries operate in an increasingly competitive, technology-driven global economy — manufacturing, agriculture, mining, logistics, healthcare, education, retail and financial services all face pressure to digitise and modernise, but many companies struggle to access affordable, relevant innovation locally.

Meanwhile, universities and innovation hubs keep producing talented graduates, research and startup ideas that rarely become commercially viable enterprises — underused capacity, limited commercialisation, and reduced industrial competitiveness. Zimbabwe has the intellectual capital to solve many of its own industrial challenges. What has been missing is a structured ecosystem that deliberately connects problems to innovators, research to markets, and startups to procurement and investment.

Objectives

Strategic

  • Strengthen collaboration between industry and academia
  • Promote commercialisation of local innovation
  • Improve industrial competitiveness through technology
  • Position Zimbabwe as a regional innovation ecosystem leader

Operational

  • Collect real business challenges across sectors
  • Facilitate solution development by innovators and universities
  • Create direct engagement with industry leaders
  • Support piloting and adoption of local innovation
